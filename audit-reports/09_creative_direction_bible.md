# Crossangle Creative Direction Bible (V2)
**Prepared by: Lead Creative Director**

**The North Star Metric:**
> *A visitor should forget they are using a website and instead feel like they are walking through the digital headquarters of an internationally recognized interior architecture studio. Every interface element should feel inevitable rather than designed.*

---

## 1. Brand Philosophy: Architecture is Light
The premium sites we emulate (Norm Architects, Vincent Van Duysen, Olson Kundig) are not memorable because of the concrete or wood they use. They are memorable because of how **light** reveals that concrete and wood. 

Our entire design language revolves around light. The UI, the colors, and the typography exist only to support the lighting system. 

## 2. The Architectural Narrative (The Journey)
We do not build "screens" or "pages" (Hero, Portfolio, Dashboard). That is SaaS thinking. We design a physical journey through physical spaces. Every interaction is moving from one room into another.

*   **Entrance (The Homepage Hero):** Awe-inspiring, dramatic, singular focus.
*   **Gallery One (The Portfolio):** High contrast, quiet, focused entirely on the art.
*   **Material Library (Methodology/Services):** Tactile, structured, inviting inspection.
*   **Studio (The App Landing):** Bright, open, collaborative energy.
*   **Consultation Room (Pricing/Contact):** Intimate, focused, hushed tones.
*   **Workspace (The Estimator):** The architect's drafting table. Glare-free, precise, utilitarian yet beautiful.

## 3. The Light System (Chapter 13)
Materials (charcoal, limestone, bronze) mean nothing without the light hitting them. Our "Themes" are actually Lighting States.

*   **Morning Light:** Crisp, high-contrast, directional. Deep, long shadows. Used for the *Entrance*. Emotion: Awakening, clarity.
*   **Gallery Light:** Focused spotlights on artwork, ambient darkness elsewhere. Zero glare. Used for *Gallery One*. Emotion: Reverence, awe.
*   **Skylight / Studio Light:** Diffused, even, shadowless illumination (like an overcast day). The canvas is a soft neutral. Used for the *Workspace (Estimator)*. Emotion: Focus, precision, professional calm.
*   **Golden Hour:** Warm, low-angle light bringing out textures (bronze, walnut). Used for the *Consultation Room*. Emotion: Warmth, trust, intimacy.
*   **Ambient Reflection:** Light bouncing off a matte surface, creating soft gradients without a direct source. Used for *Material Library*. Emotion: Tactility, curiosity.

## 4. The Rhythm System (Chapter 14)
Luxury is not spacing; luxury is rhythm. It is the breathing pattern of the layout. 
*   **Visual Silence:** Massive voids of negative space that force the user to pause. 
*   **Compression:** Tightly grouping small metadata (labels, dates, specs) to create a focal point of intense detail.
*   **Expansion:** Releasing the tension of compression with a sudden full-bleed, edge-to-edge image.
*   **The Cadence:** Large Image $\rightarrow$ Small Caption $\rightarrow$ Huge Whitespace $\rightarrow$ Small Quote $\rightarrow$ Massive Image $\rightarrow$ Tiny Label $\rightarrow$ Huge Title $\rightarrow$ Nothing $\rightarrow$ Next Room.

## 5. Signature Composition Library (Chapter 15)
Future spaces are not built from random UI blocks; they are built from this spatial vocabulary.
*   **The Reveal (Hero A):** 90% image, 10% typography. Title rests at the very bottom edge.
*   **Editorial Split:** 40% Text (left, vertically centered), 60% Image (right, bleeding off-screen). 
*   **Gallery Stack:** Asymmetrical masonry. Images of varying aspect ratios separated by vast, uneven margins.
*   **Architect Quote:** A massive serif quote floating in 80vh of pure negative space. No borders.
*   **Material Feature:** A macro-photography texture shot functioning as a background, with tiny, high-contrast structural data overlaid.
*   **Workspace Layout:** The drafting table. Strict 12-column grid, microscopic borders, highly dense but perfectly aligned data fields.

## 6. Luxury Filters (Chapter 16)
Every single design and engineering decision must pass these filters before it is committed:
1.  *Would Apple ship this?*
2.  *Would Norm Architects publish this in a monograph?*
3.  *Would this look appropriate printed in Kinfolk Magazine?*
4.  *Does this UI element distract from the photography?*
5.  *Would this interaction exist in a physical gallery space?*

If the answer to any of these is "No," the element is removed. Period.

## 7. Signature Moments
Instead of chaotic scroll animations, we curate unforgettable physical micro-experiences:
*   **The Reveal:** The initial load is a slow curtain-raise, a simulation of a light switching on in a massive dark room.
*   **The Material Hover:** When inspecting a project, the image scales imperceptibly while the surrounding surface catches a subtle glint of ambient reflection.
*   **The Workspace Transition:** Moving from the Gallery to the Estimator is a lighting shift—the dramatic spotlights cross-fade into the flat, bright, focused skylight of the drafting table.
*   **The Blueprint Calculation:** The estimator numbers tally with the weight and mechanical precision of a high-end analog watch.

## 8. Anti-Patterns (Things We Never Do)
*   **No "Web" Concepts:** No pills, no bouncing alerts, no floating glass widgets.
*   **No Unlit Materials:** A color without a lighting context is just a hex code. We do not use "flat grey"; we use "concrete in shadow".
*   **No Rushed Pacing:** If the user can scroll through the entire narrative in 3 seconds, we have failed. The rhythm must force them to slow down.
