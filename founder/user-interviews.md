# Interview plan: interior designers and lead follow-up

Assumption: the people to interview are solo or small-studio interior designers in India, per `founder/validate-idea.md`.
Assumption: you have never run a user interview, so the words to say are written out.
Assumption: the incentive amounts are Estimates. Adjust to your budget.

## 1. Interview objectives

**H1. Designers lose or delay leads because follow-up is hard, not because they lack insight.**
- Confirmed: they describe a specific recent lead that went cold, and say why (no time, no staff, forgot).
- Rejected: they reply to every lead within a day and cannot name one that went cold.

**H2. Preparing for a first consultation is a real pain that a one-page client brief would reduce.**
- Confirmed: they describe wasted first meetings, or questions they wish they had asked earlier, with a recent example.
- Rejected: they say first meetings go fine, or they already prepare using a checklist they like.

**H3. Designers already spend money or time on lead and client tools, and would pay for a narrow one.**
- Confirmed: they name a tool or paid fix they use today, with a price or hours per week.
- Rejected: they use only WhatsApp and a notebook, and have never paid for software.

## 2. Screening criteria

- **Must have:** practises interior design in India. Handles at least 2 new client inquiries a month. Runs the studio or owns the sales call. Has done a first consultation in the last 60 days.
- **Nice to have:** has tried a CRM or project tool. Works with 0 to 2 staff. Gets inquiries from Instagram or a website.
- **Disqualify:** employees of large firms (they do not buy tools). Friends and family (they will be polite). Anyone selling design software.
- **Where to find them:** Instagram DMs to studios that post projects weekly. LinkedIn search: "interior designer" plus your city, filter by "founder" or "principal". Local interior designer association groups and Facebook groups. Ask each interviewee for two names.
- **Incentive:** Estimate ₹500 UPI payment, or ₹1,000 for a 40-minute session. Pay after the call, whatever they say.

## 3. Interview script (25 minutes)

**Opening (2 minutes)**
Say: "Thank you for making time. I run an interior design studio and I am trying to understand how designers handle new inquiries. I am not selling anything. Please be blunt. If something is useless or silly, say so, because that helps me far more than politeness. May I record this for my notes only? I will not share it."
Wait for a yes before recording.

**Warm-up (3 minutes)**
1. "Tell me about your studio. How many people, and what kind of projects?"
2. "Where do most of your new inquiries come from?"
3. "How many came in last month?"

**Problem exploration (10 minutes)**
1. "Tell me about the last new inquiry you got. What happened from the first message to the first meeting?"
   - Prompts: "How long did you take to reply?" "Who replied?" "Where did you keep track of it?"
2. "Tell me about the last inquiry that never turned into a meeting. What happened?"
   - Prompts: "When did you realise it had gone quiet?" "Did you try again?"
3. "Walk me through the last time you prepared for a first meeting with a new client."
   - Prompts: "How long did it take?" "What did you look at?" "What did you wish you knew?"
4. "In that meeting, what was the first thing you still had to ask?"
   - Prompt: "How did you find that out in the end?"
5. "Tell me about a meeting that went badly. What went wrong?"
   - Prompts: "What would have changed it?"
6. "What do you use today to keep track of inquiries and clients?"
   - Prompts: "What do you pay for it?" "What annoys you about it?" "When did you last change it?"
7. "Have you ever tried to fix the follow-up or tracking problem? What did you try?"
   - Prompt: "What did it cost, in money or hours?"

**Solution exploration (5 minutes)**
1. "If you could change one thing about how inquiries are handled in your studio, what would it be?"
2. "Imagine the perfect way to prepare for a first meeting. What would you have in front of you?"
3. "What would make you refuse to try a new tool, even a free one?"
   Listen for unprompted features, their first priority, and dealbreakers such as price, Hindi or English, or WhatsApp-only habits.

**Reaction (5 minutes)**
Say: "I am testing a one-page brief for each new inquiry: what the client likes, their budget range, and what to avoid in the first meeting. It also reminds you to follow up if a lead goes quiet."
1. "Which part of that, if any, matches something you do today?"
2. "What would you do differently in your last first meeting if you had this?"
3. "What would stop you from using it?"
4. "What would you pay for it, and what would you cancel to afford it?"
Real interest sounds like specific changes to their last meeting and a price with a reason. Polite enthusiasm sounds like "nice idea" with no example.

**Closing (2 minutes)**
- "Who are two other designers who struggle with new inquiries? Could you introduce me?"
- Commitment test: "This week, would you send me one recent inquiry, with the name removed, so I can make you a sample brief? Or pay ₹500 now for a month's trial?" A yes with a date is real. "Sure, later" is not.
- Say: "Thank you. I will send your payment today, and I will share what I learn with you in two weeks."

## 4. Analysis framework

After each call, fill one row within an hour.

| # | Designer | Inquiries per month | Lost a lead recently? | First thing still had to ask | Tool and price today | Reaction (specific or polite) | Price said | Commitment made |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | | | |

- **Signal vs. noise:** a pattern is real when at least 3 of 5 interviewees give the same answer unprompted and with a recent example. One vocal outlier counts as one row, however strongly they speak.
- **Decision criteria (after 5 to 8 calls):**
  - Build: 4 of 5 describe a lost or delayed lead, 3 name a decision they would change, and 2 make the commitment.
  - Pivot: the pain is follow-up but not first-meeting preparation. Sell reminders only.
  - Kill the designer-tool idea: fewer than 2 of 8 describe the problem with an example, or nobody commits.

## 5. Red flags cheat sheet

Polite, not honest:
- "That sounds cool" with no reason.
- "I would definitely use that." Future tense is unreliable.
- "You should add feature X." They are designing for you, not describing a problem.
- They cannot describe the last time it happened. They probably do not have the problem.

Real interest:
- They describe the problem in specific, emotional terms before you mention a solution.
- They have already spent time or money trying to fix it.
- They ask when they can use it.
- They offer to introduce you to others.

Your own habit to avoid: do not explain your product in the first 20 minutes. If you catch yourself pitching, stop and ask "Tell me about the last time."
